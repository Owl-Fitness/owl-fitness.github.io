What OwlyGo is

OwlyGo is a general wellness coach for an active week: strength training at a gym or at home, plus walking, cycling, yoga, stretching and the other activities you choose. It's for people 18 or older with an iPhone and an Apple Watch or another device that records sleep and heart rate to Apple Health. It isn't a medical device, and it doesn't diagnose or treat any condition. Its plan comes from fixed rules based on general exercise guidance, not from medical advice, physical therapy or a personal coach.

Assumption of Risk and Injury Waiver

Exercise carries inherent risks. Strength training means moving weights and your own body under load. Walking, running and cycling bring traffic, uneven ground, weather and falls. Yoga, stretching and flow sessions mean holding and moving through positions that can strain muscles and joints. These risks include muscle, joint and back injury, dropped weights, falls, collisions, heat illness, cardiovascular distress and other injury, some of which can be serious, permanent or fatal. You decide whether and how to move, which activities, weights, routes and positions to choose, and whether to use a spotter, a helmet or other safety equipment, and you assume all of these risks. Talk to your doctor before you start, and if you have pain, dizziness or concerns. Stop if something feels wrong.

Emergencies, readings and suggestions

OwlyGo doesn't monitor you and can't call for help. If you have chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath or any other emergency, stop and call 911 or your local emergency number. OwlyGo's readings, alerts and suggestions, including moves, minutes, weights, rest times and protein, drink and bedtime targets, come from fixed rules and the data in Apple Health, which can include readings from your Apple Watch and from other devices or apps you choose, such as a WHOOP or Oura. That data can be wrong or missing, or arrive late, for example if a device is loose or off overnight, so the plans and targets built from it can be wrong too. No alert doesn't mean you're well. The maker of OwlyGo isn't responsible for data from Apple or from any other device or app. They aren't medical or dietary advice, and no result is promised. When you're unsure, choose a lighter option. Ask your doctor or a dietitian before following the nutrition targets if you have a medical condition such as kidney disease or diabetes, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating.

Your health and the health check

Answer the health check honestly, and update it in Settings if your health changes; OwlyGo doesn't check your answers. It is based on the PAR-Q+ and is asked again every 12 months. Depending on your answers, OwlyGo plans your normal week, a lighter plan, or no moves until you confirm that your doctor has said exercise, including strength training, is OK for you. That confirmation is yours to give, and OwlyGo relies on it. Doing more than OwlyGo suggests is your choice and your risk.

Equipment, form and surroundings

You choose your equipment and where you move, and you're responsible for checking they're safe and suit you: that weights are secured, benches and racks are stable, your bike and helmet fit, your shoes suit the ground, your route and the weather are safe, and there's a clear, non-slip space for flow sessions. Learn each move gently first, follow your gym's rules and the rules of the road, and watch your surroundings rather than your screen.
